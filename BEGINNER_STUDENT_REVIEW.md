# DataPath: complete beginner walkthrough

Reviewed September 20, 2026. Persona: a student who has never studied data, does not know SQL or Python, has no chosen data career, and can study three hours per week.

Method: scenario-based review of the current application code and its displayed copy. Browser access was denied because the admin-enforced security policy could not be verified. These are product-design findings and predicted beginner reactions, not observed clicks, rendered-layout findings or real student research. No learner progress was changed.

## Scenario and feedback

### 1. I arrive on the homepage
The headline gives me a reason to explore. The small retail challenge is approachable: I can exclude the pending order and find 180 without knowing a programming language. Immediate explanation is a good first success.

Missing: a plain-language explanation of data work, what I will learn in the first session, and an explicit message that coding experience is unnecessary. The demo ends with feedback but does not directly offer a continuation of the same task into a guided lesson. Add a “Learn how to do this with a table” action after success.

### 2. I select Find my path
I see 18 career choices. The “Not sure yet? Find my fit” option is helpful, but I must notice and open it. I do not yet understand the difference between analysis, engineering and governance, or know how much coding I enjoy.

Change: offer “I’m completely new” as a primary starting route. Ask concrete questions about activities I enjoy, show two or three explained suggestions, and keep the full catalogue as an optional expansion. Add “Explore before choosing” for industry selection; explain that the choice is reversible and that examples will change.

### 3. I describe my starting point and pace
The experience selector supports “Just starting,” but I still encounter 28 tool/skill interests and self-assessment controls for required skills. These are optional or default to New, yet the interface asks me to process unfamiliar names. A completion deadline also asks me to estimate a journey I do not understand.

Change: hide tool interests and self-assessment behind “I already have some experience” for new learners. Ask only available weekly time, offer a suggested pace, and frame the first goal as a small completed task rather than a career-ready deadline. Preserve the detailed controls for experienced learners.

### 4. I enter my workspace
The next-session focus is a useful improvement. However, ten main destinations plus settings ask me to understand the whole platform immediately. Proof ledger, AI coach, projects and career tools are more useful after I have completed some work.

Change: give beginners three primary destinations: My next lesson, Practise, My progress. Put the remaining destinations under Explore. Keep the full workspace available. Introduce a short first-session checklist and clearly say what action saves progress.

### 5. I open my first lesson
Eighteen topics have authored explanations, examples and common mistakes, while much of the broader topic catalogue still uses generic guidance. Terms such as grain, aggregation, alias, edge case and validation assume vocabulary I have not learned. Objectives asking me to build an example and test an edge case can feel like assignments before instruction.

Change: finish one coherent zero-to-first-result course. Start with rows, columns, values, sorting, filtering and asking a business question. Use “watch one example → finish a partly completed example → solve a similar task.” Explain technical terms where they first appear. Make official documentation and video search supplementary; a search-results link does not select a beginner-appropriate lesson for me.

### 6. I attempt a lab
Industry context, executable checks, hints and error coaching are valuable. But a prompt asking me to define solve(rows), write a formula, or provide artifact links and validation can still be too large a first step. Available features do not yet guarantee a guided learning sequence.

Change: add a guided mode with one action at a time, annotated starter code, input-to-output explanation and a small visible sample. After an error, point to the relevant concept and offer a smaller retry. Offer independent mode once I understand the pattern. Make it clear which answers are automatically checked and which are self-reviewed.

### 7. I finish and return another day
I need a human-readable account of what I achieved, where I stopped and what to do next. Topic percentages alone do not tell me what I can now do. Guest storage is mentioned, but the practical consequence of changing browsers deserves explanation at the moment I save meaningful work.

Change: show “You filtered completed orders and checked the total,” followed by “Next: group results by category.” Add an optional confidence check, a short recap on return and plain-language save/export information. Do not imply that finishing a plan proves job readiness.

## What I would prioritise

1. A 10–15 minute guided first win connected to the landing challenge.
2. A beginner onboarding shortcut that postpones career/tool complexity.
3. A coherent foundations course with inline vocabulary explanations.
4. Guided Practice Lab mode with graduated help and clear feedback.
5. A simple beginner workspace and a useful return-session recap.

## Acceptance checks for a later hands-on test

- A new student can explain what data work means after the welcome screen.
- They can start without choosing a technical tool or rating unknown skills.
- They can complete a first task without an external tutorial or account.
- They can explain why the answer is correct, not just reproduce a number.
- They can find their saved work and next action after reopening the app.
- Test the rendered experience separately at mobile and desktop sizes, in both themes and using a keyboard. This review cannot certify those properties.

Overall assessment: the product offers a broad learning workspace, but the zero-knowledge student needs a more deliberately taught beginning. The highest-value next improvement is one complete guided beginner journey, not another destination in the navigation.
